A body is only as good as the decisions made before the first sheet is cut. Most of the expensive mistakes we see at the workshop were not welding problems — they were specification problems.

## Start with the load, not the body

Write down what the truck actually carries: the material, the density, the weight per trip and how it is loaded. Sand and ballast are heavy and abrasive; maize and fertiliser are lighter but need volume; timber needs length and a low deck. Two trucks with the same chassis can need completely different bodies.

## Check the chassis rating

The body, the load and the fuel all count against the chassis rating. A high-sided body that looks right can push a truck over its legal weight once it is full. We weigh the chassis and work back from the permitted payload, not from what fits visually.

## Deck height and wheel clearance

Deck height affects loading, stability and how the truck behaves on a bad road. A low deck is easier to load; too low and the body fouls the tyres or the exhaust. We measure the wheel travel and the chassis rails before fixing the sub-frame height.

## Match the body to the route

A body that works on the Mombasa Road run may not survive a murram access road five times a day. Suspension, mount spacing and the thickness of the floor all change with the route. Tell the fabricator where the truck actually goes.

## Look at the joints before the paint

Ask to see a unit under construction. Look at how the body is mounted to the chassis, whether the sub-frame is braced, and whether the panels are welded or riveted. Paint hides a lot; the joints do not.
