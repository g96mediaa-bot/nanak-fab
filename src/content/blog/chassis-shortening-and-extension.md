Changing a wheelbase is common when a truck is repurposed, but it is structural work. Here is what it involves.

## Why shorten or extend

A prime mover becomes a tipper, a long chassis gets a shorter body, or a body is moved back to improve weight distribution. In each case the frame has to be cut or extended and then reinforced properly.

## The cut

The chassis is measured from the rear axle and marked on a jig, not by eye. Wiring, brake lines and fuel lines are moved clear before any cutting, and the cut is square to the frame.

## The joint

A butt joint is plated inside and outside, with the plate shaped to avoid a hard stress point at the end. Welding is done in a sequence that keeps the frame straight, and the rails are checked for twist afterwards.

## Cross-members and mounts

Shortening usually means moving a cross-member and re-drilling body mounts. Extension means adding one. Either way the body has to sit on rated mounts at the right spacing.

## Alignment and road test

The finished chassis is checked for straightness and the truck is road-tested before the body goes on. A frame that is out by a few millimetres shows up as tyre wear and a crooked body.

Bring the vehicle in for measurement before you commit to a body. The wheelbase decision comes first.
