Anyone can make a panel shiny for a month. The difference between a good paint job and a great one is what happened before the spray gun came out.

## Bare metal, properly cleaned

Grease, rust and old paint all have to go. We strip to bare metal where the surface is suspect, treat the rust, and neutralise the surface before primer. Primer over contamination is a failure waiting to happen.

## Filler, used thinly

Filler is for levelling small imperfections, not for rebuilding a panel. Deep dents are pulled or replaced first. A thick filler layer shrinks, cracks and shows a shadow under the paint.

## Blocking and priming

The primer is blocked flat in stages, with a guide coat so the low spots show up. This is slow, dusty work and it is the reason a finished panel looks straight in daylight rather than only under the workshop lights.

## Seams and edges

Seams are sealed, edges are painted, and the back of the panel gets coverage too. Rust starts where the paint stopped short.

## The booth and the mix

Two-pack paint needs the right ratio, the right temperature and a clean booth. We match the colour to the vehicle or a sample, then let it cure properly before reassembly.

Ask what prep is included when you compare quotes. If the answer is vague, the cheap quote is probably skipping the part that matters.
