Tippers fail in predictable places. A short monthly check catches most of them before they turn into a roadside stoppage.

## The hoist and hydraulics

Check the ram for weeping seals, look at the hose routing where it passes the cross-member, and make sure the tank level is right. A hose that rubs on a sharp edge will fail eventually — re-route or sleeve it now rather than on site.

## Hinges and tailgate

Grease the tailgate hinges and check the pins for play. A tailgate that drops unevenly puts a twisting load into the body sides. Replace worn pins before they oval the holes.

## Floor and side wear

Look at the floor under the point where the load lands. A wear plate is cheaper than a new floor. Check the bottom of the side panels and the top rail for cracks, especially on bodies that carry rock or ballast.

## Body mounts and sub-frame

Check the mount bolts for looseness and the sub-frame for cracks near the hoist mounting. A cracked mount shows up as a noise or a lean before it becomes a failure.

## Lights and guards

Tipper lights live in a hard place. Check the rear loom, the guards and the mud flaps, and make sure the reversing alarm still works.

Bring the unit in if anything is borderline. A day in the workshop is cheaper than a failed hoist on a site with a loaded body.
