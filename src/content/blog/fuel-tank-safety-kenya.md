A fuel tank is the one body where a shortcut can hurt people. These are the details we will not leave out.

## Baffles

A part-full tank of diesel moves. Without internal baffles, that movement loads the chassis, the mounts and the driver. Baffles are full-depth and welded, not tacked.

## Filling, venting and drainage

Every compartment needs a lockable filler, a vent that works when the tank is full, and a drain that can be operated safely. A vent that blocks turns a fill into a pressure test.

## Earthing

Static is a real ignition source. Earthing points front and rear, with a clean path to the chassis, are part of the build — not an accessory fitted later.

## Welds and testing

The shell is welded, inspected and pressure-tested before paint. A tank that weeps at a seam is a tank that has to come back; testing now is cheaper than a recovery later.

## Signage and fittings

Hazard markings, a fire extinguisher mount and a lockable cabinet are specified with the customer. The finished unit should look like a fuel tanker from every angle, because that is what other road users need to see.

Ask for the test record when you collect. Any fabricator building fuel tanks should be able to show it.
