An accident repair is part panel work and part paperwork. Knowing the sequence makes the whole thing less stressful.

## Assessment and scope

The assessor agrees the visible damage and the repair method. We strip the damaged area so hidden damage — a bent chassis horn, a cracked mounting, a distorted inner panel — is found before the repair starts rather than after the paint goes on.

## Straightening

Cabs and frames are pulled and measured, not just hammered straight. If the chassis is out, the body will never sit right and the tyres will wear unevenly. This is the stage that decides whether the repair lasts.

## Panels and preparation

Damaged panels are beaten where the metal is sound and replaced where it is not. Welds are dressed, seams sealed and the surface blocked flat. Preparation is most of the finish.

## Paint

We match the colour to the vehicle or a sample panel and paint in a booth. Two-pack paint needs the right primer and the right flash-off time; rushing it is how a repair looks tired after a year.

## Reassembly and handover

Trim, lights, glass and fittings go back on, the alignment is checked, and the repair is photographed at each stage. Those photographs are what the insurer and the fleet owner see.

If the vehicle cannot be driven, send photographs and the chassis number and we will tell you what to do next.
